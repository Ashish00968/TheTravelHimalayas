# Himalayan Altitude Safety & Medical Protocol Compliance

This skill ensures that all health and safety guidance published on **Discover Himalayan Trails** meets the highest clinical standards of wilderness medicine (UIAA, Himalayan Rescue Association, and Wilderness Medical Society).

---

## 1. The Three Golden Rules of High Altitude

Any route or article referencing elevations above 3,000m must emphasize:
1. **Rule 1**: Any sickness at altitude is Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) until proven otherwise.
2. **Rule 2**: Never ascend with symptoms of AMS.
3. **Rule 3**: If symptoms worsen or fail to improve, descend immediately (minimum 500m to 1,000m).

---

## 2. Altitude Illness Classification

### Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS):
- **Diagnostic Criteria**: Lake Louise Score (Headache + at least one of: Nausea/Vomiting, Fatigue/Weakness, Dizziness/Lightheadedness, Insomnia).
- **Intervention**: Stop ascent, rest, hydrate (3–4L/day), Acetazolamide (Diamox) under medical advice.

### High Altitude Pulmonary Edema (HAPE):
- **Symptoms**: Severe breathlessness at rest, persistent dry cough progressing to pink frothy sputum, cyanosis, bubbling chest rales.
- **Action**: Immediate descent, supplemental oxygen, portable hyperbaric bag (Gamow bag), Nifedipine if prescribed. HAPE kills quickly without descent.

### High Altitude Cerebral Edema (HACE):
- **Symptoms**: Ataxia (loss of motor coordination, inability to walk heel-to-toe in a straight line), confusion, altered mental state, severe lethargy.
- **Action**: Evacuate down immediately, Dexamethasone, oxygen. Medical emergency.

---

## 3. Acclimatization Rate Standards

- Above 3,000m: Sleeping altitude should not increase by more than 500m per day.
- Every 3–4 days: Include a dedicated acclimatization rest day ("Climb high, sleep low").
